Slot 02 · Nutanix · Dec 2024 – Oct 2025 · Technical lead

Keeping customers through VMware’s licensing shake-up

VMware’s new licensing forced about 2,000 enterprise customers running Nutanix on VMware to pick one full stack. I led Nutanix Move’s in-place ESXi-to-AHV conversion across three teams, built for zero downtime and shipped on time. About half of those customers stayed with Nutanix.

Mission

The problem

Many Nutanix customers ran a mixed stack: Nutanix software on top of VMware’s ESXi hypervisor. After Broadcom acquired VMware, it changed the licensing so customers had to buy the complete package. Partial subscriptions were gone.

That left about 2,000 enterprise customers with a choice: go all-in on VMware, or move fully to Nutanix. With the new pricing, many wanted to stay with Nutanix. The blocker was the move itself: switching every workload from ESXi to Nutanix’s own hypervisor, AHV, without downtime.

Nutanix needed a product that made that switch seamless, and needed it before customers made their decision.

Upgrades picked

What I did

  • DecisionGot leadership buy-in first

    Directors and VPs endorsed the project on one number: 2,000 enterprise customers we wanted to keep.

  • DecisionLet customers set the requirements

    Heard their migration worries, then pinned down exactly what they needed and which trade-offs they would accept.

  • ProcessBriefed all three teams up front

    Explained the impact on retention, shared the leadership mandate, and asked for every objection early.

  • ProcessBuilt a common base, then split the work

    Agreed one foundation across teams, then divided execution into parts each team could own.

  • DeliveryBeta in 2–3 months

    From the customer’s ask to a working beta, refined from their feedback before release.

  • DeliveryWidened the bottleneck

    Added two contractors and two test clusters, about 15% more QA capacity. Delivery cycles got about 20% shorter.
