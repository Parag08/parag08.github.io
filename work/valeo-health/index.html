Valeo Health · UAE · 2026 · Product management & operations

From rising fleet costs to an adopted routing product

Turning an invisible fleet-cost problem into a dispatch tool the operations team moved onto within a week.

Context

Valeo Health sends nurse–driver pairs to patients' homes for short visits: sample collection, IV therapy and injections. Orders arrive on a partner booking platform and go to whichever provider claims them first. Ops claimed them by hand in Excel, and idle pairs waited at the office between visits, so the fleet absorbed distance, fuel and time nobody could see clearly.

My scope

I led the financial diagnosis, worked with the operations team on the decisions that mattered, built Logistics Valeo, and built the Valeo Smart Tracker prototype.

Approach

The cost baseline came first, so the problem had a number rather than only a narrative. The tool was then designed around the people running the routes: an acceptance engine that claims the orders the fleet can serve, a live day view, roster auto-allocation, standby hubs for idle pairs, capacity planning and metrics.

It went live on 1 August 2026. The first days had many issues; daily catch-ups with ops for 14 days fixed them, and ops moved fully onto the tool within a week. When ops pushed back, the auto-accept lead time moved from 3 h to 24 h and night hours stopped counting.

Measured from 16 August to 16 September over 29 clean days, km per order fell from 60 to 45.3 (−24.6%). Daily order volume rose over the same period, and denser days cost less per order, so the claim is that km per order fell about 25% after launch, not that hubs alone cut it. The saving is valued at the marginal cost per km, since salaries and rental don't fall when cars drive less.

Separately, I specified and built Valeo Smart Tracker — a working prototype for medication and health-habit adherence, with a daily care plan, refill alerts, and streaks that motivate without punishing a missed day.
